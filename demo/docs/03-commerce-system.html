Haya Commerce System Guide

This guide explains how Haya can move from helping a shopper discover a product to helping the right party sell it, deliver it, handle a return, and account for the outcome. It is written for product, operations, finance, and store-management teams. It explains the business purpose of each option and field without requiring technical knowledge.

Read this first: catalog connection and commerce are separate. A connected Shopify, WooCommerce, custom-feed, or scraped catalog makes products available for discovery. It does not by itself let Haya take payment, place an order with the store, track a referral sale, or arrange delivery.

1. Commerce in plain language

Haya can support three commercial relationships. In each one, a shopper sees a product in Haya, but the party taking payment and delivering the item may differ. Haya keeps those choices explicit so a shopper is never led to believe that a product can be bought or delivered in a way the store has not approved.

TermPlain-language meaningWhy it matters
StoreThe merchant or source business that owns, supplies, or fulfils products.Each store has its own commercial settings, shipping rules, policies, costs, and responsibilities.
BuyerThe shopper who wants the product.The buyer sees one clear next action: checkout in Haya, buy on the store website, or request help from the store.
CatalogThe product information Haya can search and display: names, variants, prices, stock signals, images, and links.A catalog is product data. It is not proof that a product is available to sell through Haya.
Sales modelThe agreed way a shopper completes a purchase or request.It determines who charges the buyer, owns customer support, issues documents, and earns a commission or margin.
ProcurementHow Haya asks a source store to provide an item after a Haya Checkout sale.It can be handled by an operations person or, only when verified, by the store's order API.
FulfillmentWho receives, packs, and ships the item to the buyer.It is independent of the sales model: a store can ship directly or send goods through a Haya warehouse.
QuoteA time-limited, server-calculated offer for the selected items, address, shipping, tax, and currency.It prevents browser-entered prices, old shipping rates, or changed product details from being charged as if they were current.
Supplier costThe agreed amount Haya owes the source store for an item.It is private commercial data, separate from the buyer's retail price and not exposed publicly.
CommissionHaya's percentage earnings for a confirmed referral or assisted-lead sale.It is based on the eligible item subtotal after discounts, excluding tax and shipping, and can be reversed after a refund.
SettlementThe controlled process of calculating and paying what is owed to a supplier.It keeps supplier cost, delivery costs, fees, reserves, refunds, and payouts auditable rather than changing historical orders.

PartyCore responsibility

2. The decisions each store makes

A store does not choose one universal "commerce type." It makes several independent decisions. Keeping them separate avoids common mistakes, such as assuming that a catalog connection automatically makes a store checkout-ready.

DecisionQuestion it answersChoices in HayaImportant distinction
Business/store typeWhat kind of business is this store?The store profile selects its category and store type for classification and discovery.This identifies the business. It does not decide how a buyer pays or receives products.
Catalog sourceHow do product details enter Haya?Manual entry, Shopify API, WooCommerce API, custom product feed, or assisted scraper ingestion.This brings in product data. It is not a promise that Haya can place supplier orders.
Sales modelWhere does the buyer complete the commercial action?Catalog Only, Haya Checkout, Store Referral, or Assisted Lead / Order.A store may be configured for more than one model, but a product page presents one eligible action at a time.
Procurement modeHow does Haya obtain an item after Haya Checkout?Operator-assisted or verified API procurement.API catalog reading alone is not API order writing.
Fulfillment methodWho physically gets the item to the buyer?Store Direct or Haya Warehouse.Fulfillment is separate from sales. The same commercial model can use either route when eligible.
Shipping methodWhich delivery options can a buyer choose?Flat/free/weight/value/quantity/local/pickup methods, or carrier-calculated options.Each shipping method has its own matching rules, price, delivery estimate, and availability.
Policy and financial settingsHow do tax, returns, delivery promises, supplier payout, and exceptions work?Store-specific tax, return, delivery, and settlement policies.These settings are snapshotted on an order so later edits do not rewrite history.

A simple mental model

Use the sequence below when assessing a new store: first decide whether its products can be discovered; next decide how the buyer should act; then decide how the product is sourced and delivered; finally approve the commercial policies that make the process safe to operate.

Store profile -> Catalog -> Sales model -> Procurement -> Fulfillment -> Shipping/tax/returns -> Settlement

Catalog Only

Catalog Only is the safe default for a newly connected store. Its products can appear in search and product discovery, but Haya makes no claim that it can take payment, order the item, or arrange delivery. Use this when the commercial agreement is not yet approved or the required workflow is not ready.

Haya Checkout

Haya owns the buyer-facing order experience. It groups a buyer's cart by store, calculates a quote on the server, creates one store sub-order for each source store, and keeps the buyer informed when availability changes. Haya retail price and supplier cost are distinct commercial values; supplier cost remains private.

In the current investor-demo configuration, payment is a mock Haya payment page. It records a simulated successful payment and supports the lifecycle demonstration, but it never contacts a bank, captures real money, or represents a live PSP transaction. Live payment remains gated on a selected payment provider, market/currency support, settlement approvals, and provider verification.

Store Referral

The buyer leaves Haya for a store's approved destination. Haya records a unique click identifier and campaign details, then the merchant or affiliate network must report the eventual order through an approved, signed callback. A click alone is not a sale; UTM tags alone are not sufficient proof of a sale.

Haya supports affiliate-network and direct-merchant reporting models. A store can select ArabClicks, Awin, Rakuten Advertising, impact.com, Admitad, or CJ Affiliate, or use a direct Shopify/WooCommerce merchant callback. Each store needs its own verified destination, commission rate, attribution window, and conversion method.

Assisted Lead / Order

This is a request for help, not a paid Haya order. The buyer chooses a product, variant, and quantity, provides the minimum contact and delivery details needed, and explicitly agrees to share those details with the store. The store progresses the request in its work queue, completes payment and delivery outside Haya, then records a confirmed merchant order if a commission is due.

4. End-to-end business flows

4.1 Store onboarding and product readiness

  1. Create the store profile: name, business type, country, currency, contacts, website, address, location, and working hours.
  2. Choose a catalog source. Add products manually or connect a supported source for product synchronization.
  3. Review imported products, variants, images, active status, weights, and prices. Imported data remains subject to store review.
  4. Choose Catalog Only until a commercial model is approved. A catalog connection does not change this choice.
  5. For a commercial model, configure its required fields, capability evidence, shipping/policy settings, and readiness blockers.
  6. Use the readiness result to decide whether the store can be enabled for a buyer action. A saved draft is not the same as a ready, launchable store.

4.2 Haya Checkout: buyer to delivery

  1. The buyer adds eligible products, variants, and quantities to a Haya cart.
  2. The buyer provides a saved or inline structured delivery address and chooses one valid shipping option for each store sub-order.
  3. Haya validates the product, variant, availability, currency, private supplier cost, delivery address, shipping rate, tax profile, and commercial configuration. It returns a versioned, expiring quote calculated by the server.
  4. Haya creates one parent buyer order and one store sub-order for each source store. Product details, prices, addresses, shipping, tax, delivery promise, and relevant policies are copied into the order record so later catalog edits cannot change a historical order.
  5. For the current demo, the buyer completes a mock payment. For a live launch, this point must be a verified payment-provider authorization/capture workflow rather than a browser redirect.
  6. Haya operations or a verified supplier-order integration asks each source store to accept or reject its sub-order. The request carries the required item, selected variant, quantity, recipient, and delivery information.
  7. If all stores accept, each accepted sub-order proceeds to its chosen fulfillment route. The store records direct shipment details, or the warehouse receives the goods before outbound delivery.
  8. If a store rejects or cannot supply an item, Haya pauses the parent order and asks the buyer to either continue with the available store sub-orders or cancel the entire order. Haya never silently converts a multi-store order into a partial order.
  9. Shipment, delivery, returns, refunds, documents, supplier ledger entries, and payouts remain linked to the relevant store sub-order and its event timeline.
Multi-store rule: one cart can create several store sub-orders. A buyer still sees a single parent order, but each store has its own supplier response, shipping choice, delivery status, refund, and settlement record.

4.3 Store Referral: click to commission

  1. The buyer chooses Buy on store website.
  2. Haya records a secure, opaque click identifier, product/store reference, timestamp, locale, and campaign details.
  3. Haya redirects only to the store's pre-verified destination or approved affiliate tracking template. It does not accept an arbitrary destination from the browser.
  4. The store or network preserves the Haya click identifier through its checkout and reports a signed conversion event with merchant order reference, amount, currency, status, and refund information.
  5. Haya verifies the callback, rejects duplicates, checks the configured attribution window, and creates a pending commission entry using the store's configured rate.
  6. After the agreed confirmation point, the commission is approved. Cancellation, chargeback, returned items, or full/partial refund reverses or adjusts the ledger entry.

4.4 Assisted Lead / Order: request to conversion

  1. The buyer chooses Request this item, selects the product/variant/quantity, and supplies contact details and optional delivery context.
  2. Before the request is shared, the buyer gives explicit consent. The consent time and product snapshot are retained with the lead.
  3. The store sees the lead in its in-app queue and progresses it through New -> Contacted -> Quoted/Accepted -> Converted / Declined / Expired.
  4. The store completes commercial payment and delivery outside Haya. The lead is never shown as a paid Haya order.
  5. When a sale is confirmed, the store records the merchant order reference and eligible subtotal. Haya records the configured commission once; duplicate confirmations are blocked.
  6. Unanswered active leads expire according to the configured policy (30 days by default) and cannot earn commission after expiry.

5. Delivery and warehouse flows

Fulfillment answers a physical question: where does the item go after the store accepts it? The sales model describes the commercial relationship; fulfillment describes the movement of goods.

Fulfillment routePhysical journeyOperational ownerWhen it is appropriate
Store DirectSource store -> buyerThe store supplies tracking and shipment updates. Haya coordinates the buyer experience for Haya Checkout; the merchant owns it for referral/lead sales.Initial/default route when the store can pack and ship directly.
Haya WarehouseSource store -> Haya warehouse -> buyerHaya operations receives, inspects, records inventory movement, picks/packs, and ships outbound.Only when an active default warehouse, domestic route, delivery option, and operations process are configured.

5.1 Store-direct fulfillment

  1. Store accepts its supplier sub-order.
  2. Store packs the item and records carrier, tracking number or tracking URL, and shipment status.
  3. The buyer can see shipment progress: created, in transit, delivered, failed, or returned.
  4. Delivery confirmation triggers the eligible downstream processes, such as settlement availability and return eligibility.
  5. Delivery exceptions are recorded for operations rather than hidden. The responsibility for buyer support follows the sales model.

5.2 Haya warehouse fulfillment

  1. Checkout creates an inbound transfer for the store sub-order and the expected quantities.
  2. The store sends goods to the approved Haya warehouse and records supplier-to-warehouse tracking.
  3. At receiving, Haya operations records the quantity received, accepted, damaged, or short for every item.
  4. Accepted units create an auditable inventory receipt. Damaged or missing units remain exceptions; an outbound shipment is blocked until the buyer decision is resolved.
  5. If only some goods are acceptable, the buyer can approve shipment of available items with the difference refunded, or cancel that store sub-order. The buyer is not silently sent fewer items.
  6. Haya creates an outbound shipment and tracks delivery to the buyer. Returns can later be restocked, held as damaged, returned to supplier, or disposed of with an audit record.

When no shipping methods exist, the system currently retains a zero-shipping compatibility path; this should be treated as an explicit commercial decision before a live launch.

7. Lifecycle, records, and automatic safeguards

7.1 Haya Checkout order lifecycle

The buyer sees a parent order. Operations works with one store sub-order per source store. Each transition creates an event record, which keeps the decision trail visible.

Quote -> Order created -> Payment recorded -> Supplier pending -> Supplier accepted / rejected -> Processing -> Shipped -> Delivered -> Return/refund if needed

  • Supplier rejected: the parent order becomes buyer_decision_required. The buyer chooses continue with available items or cancel all; no silent partial fulfillment.
  • Buyer-decision deadline: default 48 hours. On expiry, the automated process cancels the order and refunds mock transactions. Live provider-specific void/refund behavior still needs provider implementation.
  • Supplier-response deadline: default 24 hours per store. The system escalates the overdue sub-order to Haya operations and can queue SMS alerts for active admins with verified phone numbers; it does not auto-reject the order.
  • Order snapshots: product, variant, supplier cost, price, address, shipping method, tax, and policy values are copied to the order to preserve what was agreed at purchase time.

7.2 Returns and refunds

Requested -> Approved / Rejected -> In transit -> Received -> Inspected -> Refunded / Replaced / Declined -> Closed

Returns require a delivered order and an active matching policy. The system checks that a buyer cannot return more than the purchased quantity. A completed return can calculate item and tax refund, original shipping refund, restocking fee, and customer-paid return shipping. It caps refunds at the remaining payment balance and creates a credit note from the refund record.

7.3 Supplier settlement

Settlement is not a modification of an old order. It is an append-only ledger of what Haya owes or recovers. After eligible delivery, the ledger can record supplier payable, supplier shipping, platform fee, reserve hold, reserve release, refund recovery, and approved manual adjustment. Payouts claim available lines, enforce the minimum payout, and record an external payment reference when marked paid.

Draft -> Pending approval -> Approved -> Scheduled -> Paid -> Closed

7.4 Assisted leads and referrals

Leads carry a separate timeline and are never payment orders. Referrals carry click and conversion records. Both commission paths use append-only entries so a confirmed conversion, cancellation, refund, or chargeback can be explained later without overwriting the original evidence.

8. Examples: problems and outcomes

Example 1: two-store checkout and one unavailable item

Situation: A buyer adds a dress from Store A and shoes from Store B. Both items create their own store sub-order under one parent order. Store A accepts, but Store B rejects because the selected shoe size is unavailable.

What Haya does: Haya does not ship the dress silently. It presents the buyer with two clear choices: continue with Store A's accepted item at the revised total, or cancel the entire order. If the buyer does not respond before the configured deadline, the order is cancelled rather than partially fulfilled without consent.

Why it matters: The buyer controls the changed purchase. Operations has an event trail showing Store B's reason, the buyer decision, and any refund.

Example 2: referral click without a confirmed sale

Situation: A shopper clicks Buy on store website, visits the merchant, but never completes checkout.

What Haya does: It records the click and campaign data but creates no commission. A commission appears only when the configured merchant or network sends a verified, eligible conversion event within the attribution window.

Why it matters: Marketing clicks are not treated as revenue. Finance can distinguish traffic from confirmed commissionable sales.

Example 3: address has no delivery option

Situation: A buyer selects a city outside the store's configured shipping zones.

What Haya does: It shows no matching shipping option and blocks checkout for that store sub-order rather than charging an invented delivery price. Operations can add a valid rate rule or leave the destination unsupported.

Why it matters: Delivery commitments are based on rules the store can actually fulfill.

Example 4: return after delivery

Situation: A buyer requests a return within the policy window. The policy says the store pays return shipping, original buyer shipping is refundable, and a 5% restocking fee applies.

What Haya does: The store/admin approves, receives, and completes the return. The refund calculation applies the policy snapshot, caps the total at the remaining payment balance, records any supplier recovery separately, and issues a linked credit note.

Why it matters: The outcome follows the policy that applied when the order was placed, not a later edited policy.

Example 5: warehouse receives damaged units

Situation: A store sends five items to Haya's warehouse. Three are accepted, one is damaged, and one is missing.

What Haya does: Warehouse staff record every item quantity and exception. Outbound shipping stays blocked until the buyer chooses to accept the available quantity with a refund for the difference, or cancel that store sub-order.

Why it matters: Inventory and financial records show the physical truth, and the buyer decides how to proceed.

9. Manager launch checklist

Use this checklist before enabling a store for any buyer action beyond Catalog Only.

  1. Confirm the store identity, country, currency, contacts, and catalog ownership.
  2. Confirm that product prices, variants, stock signals, images, weights, and source links are current enough for the intended action.
  3. Choose the sales model based on the commercial agreement, not on the catalog provider.
  4. For Haya Checkout, approve supplier costs, procurement mode, supplier response SLA, fulfillment route, shipping rules, delivery policy, tax issuer/rules, return policy, and settlement terms.
  5. For Store Referral, verify the destination, partner/network contract, click-ID propagation, signed callback, commission rate, and attribution window with a sandbox conversion.
  6. For Assisted Lead, confirm consent wording, the in-app follow-up owner, lead response process, conversion reporting owner, and expiry policy.
  7. For Store Direct, confirm packing, tracking updates, dispatch SLA, delivery-exception owner, and return handoff.
  8. For Haya Warehouse, confirm the active default warehouse, receiving staff, inspection process, domestic route, outbound delivery, inventory handling, and return disposition process.
  9. Review readiness blockers rather than bypassing them. A missing provider, supplier cost, destination, callback, or verified capability is a business risk signal.
  10. Run a controlled end-to-end test before exposing the buyer action: product selection, quote, payment mode, supplier response, delivery, return, document, and financial record.
